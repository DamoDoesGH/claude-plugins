# Partner-persona review of the Liquid YC W27 draft

Six reviewers, each an agent briefed to read draft v1 as a named current YC partner, grounded in that partner's public writing, with the deck's fact base as the founder's own claims. These are simulations of a perspective, not the people. One caveat from the research: a July 2026 report says Tom Blomfield took leave from YC to join Anthropic, so treat his review as "the regulated-fintech-builder seat" rather than a prediction of who reads the form.

## Verdicts

| Reviewer (seat) | Verdict | One line |
|---|---|---|
| Garry Tan (President and CEO) | Borderline, lean interview | "A deck with brackets in it, not an application." Founder-market fit is real; evidence is zero; the ask to YC hurts. |
| Jared Friedman (Managing Partner, ideas and mechanisms) | Borderline, lean interview | Genuinely new attack on a tarpit, but the application hides the hole the founder's own notes admit. |

| Jon Xu (GP, asset management and index products) | Borderline, lean interview | Demand side and data business asserted, not shown; wants twenty minutes to see if he knows the difference. |
| Gustaf Alströmer (GP, growth and first users) | Borderline, lean interview | A design document: not one founder has signed, no employee has tapped, no buyer has bid. |

Six for six on the same verdict. Nobody said pass; nobody said straight interview. The interview is decided by what is in "How far along are you" on 2 November.

## What every reviewer agreed on

1. **Founders Pledge is the application.** All six independently called it the single most relevant fact: a one-page optional document signed at formation, 2,300 signers, $13.6bn. v1 had it in the idea section as one line of biography. v2 leads with it.
2. **Targets are not progress.** The ninety-day plan was written in September. All six want it replaced with results: founders asked, yes, yes in writing, no, and the objection. Several said a plan with targets reads worse than nothing because it shows the founder chose design over conversations.
3. **Drop the ask for YC paperwork.** Garry gave three reasons: neutrality (YC endorsing a commercial counterparty taking 2%), conflict (YC's funds hold these shares and a note that pre-wires ROFR changes YC's own rights), and that it signals the business depends on a favor from the room. Harj and Gustaf said the same: "your batch is my distribution" is the wrong read. v2 says explicitly "I am not asking for that" and gives the founder-driven path.
4. **Two false claims had to go.** "No sale ever needs a signature again" and "existing investors never see a price they didn't set." The first is contradicted by the Series A survivability problem in your own notes; the second because the cleared cap is visible and 409A appraisers will use it. Jared, Tom and Jon each caught both.
5. **Decide the cofounder answer.** "Yes [confirm]" followed by "I'd rather go alone" reads as undecided. Name the Acorns operator and their status. Harj's interview question two is "why is he a hire and not your cofounder?"
6. **Cut "seven engineers".** A hiring plan standing in for a founding team, and the opposite of how 2026 YC companies build. The batch should produce a cleared trade on a partner broker-dealer with at most one engineer.
7. **Cut "up to three times more equity".** Your own speaker notes say closer to 2x. Use the Adcock numbers only.

## Where the seats differed, and what v2 did about it

- **The mechanism.** Jared and Tom go deepest. Tom: "cash now, price later, up to a cap" is a prepaid variable forward on restricted stock, itself a security; to the IRS it is a sale today or a loan; the two-year fallback with a buyer-paid outside valuation is "a one-way option adjudicated by an appraiser you also employ." Jared: the two-year fallback is the base case not the edge case (61% of 2020-21 unicorns never raised again), and the simulator number came from models bidding against models, not humans. v2 characterizes the instrument honestly in "What is your company going to make", says who holds the two-year risk, and promises securities and tax opinions before the first auction. It does not try to resolve the characterization in the application; naming it is what YC rewards.
- **The warranty.** Tom: a promise to indemnify a third party against a contingent loss for a fee is insurance, backed by a company with no capital. Jared: at $2,400 a year for 8,000 companies, a human appraiser signs 8,000 sets of unaudited startup financials for $19m total with unbounded liability. v2 caps the refund (you choose the formula) and keeps the appraiser sign-off.
- **Demand.** Jon's seat is the most skeptical: at seed-to-B nobody buys the asset class directly, the only basket buyer is a formula bidder, and the cleared cap is a ceiling on a forward, not a mark. He lists the first ten buyer types (Jeff's LP as formula bidder, existing funds topping up pro rata, Industry Ventures-type specialists, ESO Fund/Secfi/Quid whose product already looks like a capped forward, AngelList and Hiive SPV leads, family offices, a multi-stage fund buying a toehold, the company itself) and expects ordinary seed-to-B common to clear 40-60% below the last preferred post-money. He also flagged that insiders bidding first in a sealed auction is a winner's curse for outsiders. v2 replaces "most investors want the asset class" with the formula-bidder framing and drops index/ETF entirely.
- **Revenue model.** Tom: "nobody at YC believes a year-three model of $155m," lead with $80k per median auction and $1.2m in year one. Jared: show the volume the model implies ($4.8bn, three times Forge's lifetime run rate). Jon: the $30m data line is PitchBook-tier, cut to $3-5m. v2 does all three.
- **Distribution.** Gustaf's seat: the employee viral loop is a slide, with an unmeasured conversion at each hop and a first hop that is itself a cold start; "14 of your employees want to sell" may be a message founders feel threatened by, so test the message on five founders before building around it. His seven-day plan: email 30 founders with the note attached, get five written yeses and record objections, run five buyer calls on a dated named auction, put the decoder in front of one company's employees and count, incorporate, put the prototype on a live URL, rewrite "How far along". v2's checklist is ordered by his plan.
- **The founder.** Harj's seat is the most positive on you and the most pointed about what you do with the batch: "the issue is whether he spends the batch getting signatures or shipping revision 30 of the deck. Everything in the fact base says he builds before he asks." He also wants Batelle addressed if asked (interview question five).
- **Location.** Harj flagged a bracketed "based in SF" as a weak signal if you are not actually moving. v2 gives you two honest options.

## Interview question bank (deduplicated, 30 questions from six seats)

Asked by three or more seats:
- How many founders have seen the note, how many signed, how many in writing, and what did the ones who said no say?
- Walk me through the first cleared auction: which company, who sells, who are the three buyers, who settles it, what date.
- A Series A lead strikes the note from the investor rights agreement. What happens to the employees who already sold, and have you asked a lead?
- Who is the broker-dealer partner for the first trade, and what has counsel said about tender-offer characterization and the instrument?
- A company's numbers restate 15%. How much do you pay, from what money?

Asked by one or two seats:
- Why does a buyer show up for a 15-company first window?
- Twenty companies on one date is your thesis; how do you get to twenty before a buyer will show up if YC never touches its paperwork?
- The Acorns operator: why a hire and not your cofounder?
- Can you ship a cleared trade in the batch with no hires? If not, what exactly needs an engineer you don't have?
- You could raise $3m on your name next week. What do you get from YC that money doesn't buy?
- Buyer wires $4m on Monday. Who holds the cash, who holds the shares, what is on the buyer's tax form?
- In a company that never raises again, what does the buyer own at month 25, and who decides the number?
- An auction clears 50% below the last round. What does the 409A provider do with it, and what does the founder tell the Series A lead?
- Why should an outsider bid in a sealed auction where insiders see the books and bid first? What share of cleared cash in your simulator came from non-insiders?
- Why is the SAFE the right analogy when the SAFE's counterparty was at the table and yours (the future investor) is not?
- A founder gets a message saying fourteen employees want to sell. What did the founders you tested this on say?
- What made founders sign Founders Pledge, and what did you get wrong at Batelle?
- How much capital must you reserve for the restatement warranty, and who is on the hook if you are wrong?

## Facts the reviewers wanted that would most change the verdict

In order, the single piece of evidence each seat said would move them from borderline to interview:

1. Garry: ten named founders who said yes with the note in front of them, three in writing, one startup-firm partner agreed to co-draft.
2. Jared: a Series A term sheet or IRA rider a real firm (Goodwin, Cooley, Gunderson) says they would accept carrying the note forward, or a lead on record.
3. Harj: a technical or broker-dealer cofounder named in the application.
4. Tom: a securities and tax memo, even two pages, saying what the instrument is; a named R&W insurer or reserve behind the warranty.
5. Jon: three named buyers each committing to bid a formula across a batch, discount written down; or one signed data pilot at any price.
6. Gustaf: one company where ten employees used the decoder and the founder's reaction in his own words; a named buyer committed to a specific first auction date.
